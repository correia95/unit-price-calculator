How the unit price calculator works

Supermarket packs come in awkward sizes — 375g here, 1.2kg there, a “3 for $5” deal on the end cap. The only fair way to compare them is the unit price: the cost per a fixed amount like 100g, 1kg or 1 litre. Enter each product’s price and size above and this tool does the division for you, flags the best value, and tells you how much you save.

The formula

Unit price = total price ÷ total quantity. Then multiply to a standard amount:

ExampleWorkingUnit price
$4.50 for 750g4.50 ÷ 750 × 100$0.60 per 100g ($6.00/kg)
$7.00 for 1.2kg7.00 ÷ 1200 × 100$0.58 per 100g ($5.83/kg)
3 × 400g for $5.005.00 ÷ 1200 × 100$0.42 per 100g ($4.17/kg)

Tips for comparing at the shops

  • Match the units first — convert everything to grams or millilitres in your head, or just pick the right unit in the dropdown.
  • For a multi-buy, put the single-pack size in “Size” and the number of packs in “Packs”.
  • Watch for “was / now” pricing — the unit price on the shelf label sometimes still shows the old size.
  • Cheaper per kg isn’t always the right call if you’ll throw half of it out. Buy the amount you’ll actually use.

Frequently asked questions

Does this work for millilitres and litres?

Yes — switch to Volume and compare per 100mL and per litre. Use Each / pack for things sold by count, like toilet rolls or dishwasher tablets.

Is my data saved?

Your last comparison is kept in your browser so it’s there next time. Nothing is uploaded and there’s no tracking. The “Share” button puts the comparison in the link itself.

Is unit pricing required in Australia?

Most grocery retailers must display unit prices under the Retail Grocery Industry (Unit Pricing) Code. This calculator is handy when a label is missing, unclear, or shown in a different unit to the product next to it.
